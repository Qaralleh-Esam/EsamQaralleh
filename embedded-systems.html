Embedded Systems Design

Architecture, programming and interfacing of PIC microcontroller-based systems, taught through hands-on, project-based design.

Course Description

This course introduces students to the fundamental concepts of embedded systems, focusing on the architecture, programming, and interfacing of microcontroller-based systems. Using PIC microcontrollers, students explore embedded hardware and software design, digital input/output, timers, interrupts, analog-to-digital conversion (ADC), pulse-width modulation (PWM), and serial communication. The course emphasizes practical applications through programming, simulation, and interfacing with sensors, actuators, and motors.

  • LevelUndergraduate
  • PlatformPIC 16F84A / 16F877A
  • ToolsMPLAB, mikroC PRO
  • DeliveryProject-based

Key Topics

  1. Microcontroller vs. microprocessor architecture and the PIC 8-bit family.
  2. Digital I/O, parallel ports, clocking, and switch/LED interfacing.
  3. Assembly and C (mikroC PRO) programming with MPLAB.
  4. Interrupts, timers, counters, and Capture/Compare/PWM modules.
  5. Analog-to-digital conversion and signal conditioning.
  6. Sensors, displays, keypads, and DC/stepper/servo motor drivers.

Topics Covered

Ch.TopicContent
1Introduction to Embedded SystemsMicrocontroller vs. microprocessor architecture, semiconductor memory technologies (SRAM, ROM, Flash), computer organization, and 8-bit PIC microcontroller family overview.
2PIC Mid-Range Family & 16F84A ArchitectureProgram and data memory structure, status registers, Special Function Registers (SFRs), banked RAM addressing, and configuration word settings.
3Parallel Ports, Power Supply, & Clock OscillatorsPort A/B electrical characteristics, push-pull vs. open-drain outputs, switch/LED interfacing, pull-up resistors, and RC/crystal clock oscillators.
4Starting to Program – Introduction to AssemblerSoftware development cycle, PIC 16 instruction set, MPLAB IDE, MPASM assembler, software simulation with MPSIM, and downloading code to hardware.
5Building Assembler ProgramsFlowcharts/state diagrams, conditional branching, subroutines, software delay generation, bitwise logical instructions, look-up tables (retlw/addwf PCL), macros, and mikroC PRO.
6Interrupts, Counters, & TimersHardware interrupt processing (INTCON, ISR, context saving, critical regions), Timer 0 (TMR0) module, prescalers, hardware delay loops, and Sleep mode.
7Larger Systems & PIC 16F873A/16F874A/16F877AProgram memory paging (PCLATH), expanded I/O ports (Ports A–E), peripheral allocation, and system testing/diagnostic tools (emulators, debuggers).
8Human & Physical InterfacesMatrix keypads, 7-segment display multiplexing, switch debouncing, sensors (optical, ultrasonic, Hall effect, temperature), and motor drivers (DC, H-bridge, stepper, servo).
9Taking Timing FurtherAdvanced timing features including 16-bit Timer 1, 8-bit Timer 2 (with PR2 period register and postscalers), and Capture/Compare/PWM (CCP) hardware modules.
11Data Acquisition & ManipulationAnalog-to-Digital Converters (ADC characteristics, step size, resolution), signal conditioning, voltage scaling/translation circuits, analog multiplexing, and comparators.

Interactive Simulation Labs

Browser-based PIC simulators: write assembly, run or single-step it, and watch the registers, breadboard LEDs, 7-segment display, timer and interrupts update live. The I/O Pin Lab zooms in on the hardware behind one port pin, and the Power-Up Reset Lab shows why the PIC must be held in reset at switch-on. Click a lab to open it in a new tab.

Launch simulator 18-pin · 2 banks · 1K×14 Flash

PIC16F84A Simulation Lab

The classic 18-pin PIC used throughout the course: PORTA/PORTB, TMR0, the RB0/INT interrupt and bank switching.

Open in a new tab ↗ Launch simulator 40-pin · 4 banks · 8K×14 Flash

PIC16F877 Simulation Lab

The larger 40-pin device with PORTA–PORTE, four register banks and a logic analyzer for tracing any pin.

Open in a new tab ↗ Launch lab One port pin · Q1–Q4 · switch & LED

PIC16F84A I/O Pin Lab

Look inside a single port pin. Type an instruction and watch the data latch and TRIS latch get clocked, the tri-state buffers switch on and off, and the result reach a switch or an LED. Includes the bank-select, read-modify-write and open-drain RA4 traps.

Open in a new tab ↗ Launch lab POR · PWRTE · OST · MCLR RC

PIC16F84A Power-Up Reset Lab

Watch the first milliseconds after switch-on: VDD ramping up and the crystal starting. See how the internal POR, the 72 ms power-up timer, the oscillator start-up timer and the external R-C (with diode and Rs) on MCLR keep the PIC in reset until conditions are healthy.
