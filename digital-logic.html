Digital Logic Design & Laboratory

Foundational course covering binary numbers, logic gates, Boolean simplification, combinational circuits, and finite state machines.

Course Description

Foundational course covering binary numbers, logic gates, Boolean simplification, combinational circuits, and finite state machines. Students move from number systems and Boolean algebra to the design of combinational and sequential circuits, and build and test their designs in the laboratory.

  • Course code22241
  • LevelUndergraduate
  • TypeLecture & Lab
  • Leads to22344 Microprocessors

Key Topics

  1. Number systems, binary arithmetic, and 2’s complement representation.
  2. Boolean algebra, Karnaugh maps, and logic gate minimization.
  3. Multiplexers, decoders, adders, and arithmetic circuits.
  4. Flip-flops, counters, shift registers, and Finite State Machine (FSM) design.

Topics Covered

#TopicContent
1Binary systemsNumber bases and conversions, complements, signed numbers, binary codes
2Boolean algebra & logic gatesTheorems, Boolean functions, canonical and standard forms
3Gate-level minimizationKarnaugh maps, don’t-care conditions, NAND/NOR implementation
4Combinational logicAdders, subtractors, comparators, decoders, encoders, multiplexers
5Synchronous sequential logicLatches, flip-flops, state diagrams and finite state machines
6Registers & countersShift registers, ripple and synchronous counters
7Memory & programmable logicRAM, ROM and programmable logic devices

Textbook

  • M. Morris Mano and Michael D. Ciletti, Digital Design, Pearson.

K-Map Lab

Simplify functions on a Karnaugh map step by step: Gray-code adjacency, prime implicants, essential prime implicants and the minimal cover. Then practise: draw your own groups and get instant feedback on validity, primality and minimality.
